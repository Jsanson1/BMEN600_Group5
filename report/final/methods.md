## A. Datasets

The development dataset is the PhysioNet "Gait in Parkinson's Disease" database, version 1.0.0 (gaitpdb) [S7], [S8], accessed on 5 October 2026; its 311 files match the published SHA-256 checksums. It describes 93 people with PD and 73 controls from three sub-studies, each with its own protocol: Ga, a dual-task study [S4]; Ju, a study of rhythmic auditory cueing [S6]; and Si, a study of gait speed and treadmill walking [S5], [S19]. 165 of them (93 with PD, 72 controls) have recordings, 306 in all and one to seven per person. In the usual walk, participants walked at their own pace along a corridor, for about 2 min in Ga and Si [S7] and for 100 m (four lengths of a 25 m corridor) in Ju [S6]. Eight force sensors under each foot recorded vertical force at 100 Hz, and the files give each foot's total force; a demographic table gives age, sex, height, weight, Hoehn and Yahr stage, UPDRS score and walking speed [S7]. We use each participant's usual walk (walk 01) only. In Si it is assumed to be unassisted overground walking: the database does not state its condition, and its walking speeds match the source study's unassisted condition [S19].

The external dataset is WearGait-PD [S9], [S20], collected by the US Food and Drug Administration at two Johns Hopkins sites and the VA Puget Sound Health Care System, all with the same equipment and protocol [S9]. We use its cross-sectional release (Version 1, Synapse folder syn55052683; the later longitudinal Version 2 is not used), 185 participants (100 with PD, 85 controls) [S9]: the two participant sheets, downloaded on 14 October 2026, and all 553 available files of three self-paced straight-walking tasks, downloaded on 2 November 2026 and each checked against its Synapse checksum. In SelfPace, participants make four passes across a 4.9 m instrumented walkway, starting about 1.5 m before it and turning beyond it; in SelfPace_mat they start, stop and turn on the walkway; SelfPace_matTURN has five passes, alternating the direction of the turns [S9]. Each file holds, at 100 Hz, the total force under each foot from pressure insoles with 16 sensors each, the walkway's foot contacts, and frame-by-frame labels of the task from video annotation by two expert reviewers [S9]; we use the "Walk" and "Turn" labels. [UNVERIFIED: the labels are defined in the article's Supplementary Tables S1 to S3, which the group has not yet read; the cleaning depends on what "Walk" covers.]

In both datasets, one independent case is one participant, labelled PD or control as the dataset records. A gaitpdb participant contributes one recording; a WearGait-PD participant contributes up to three task files (three for 167 participants, two for 2), whose strides are pooled. A participant's strides are never split between training and test.

## B. Participants

Because the intended use is screening people aged 50 or over, younger participants are excluded (3 in gaitpdb, 4 in WearGait-PD). The features need at least 40 clean strides on each foot in gaitpdb and 10 in WearGait-PD, whose walks are much shorter (Section D). Of gaitpdb's 162 participants aged 50 or over with a recording, one has no recorded walking speed, which locating its turns requires, and six have too few clean strides, leaving 155 (87 with PD, 68 controls). Of WearGait-PD's 181, 11 have no insole data and one has too few clean strides, leaving 169 (94 with PD, 75 controls), 7 of whom used an assistive device during testing (a cane for 5, a walker for 1, unspecified for 1). Table I (Section IV) describes them.

## C. Responsible use

gaitpdb is openly available under the Open Data Commons Attribution License 1.0 and is cited as its terms require [S7], [S8]. WearGait-PD is released under CC BY 4.0, and access requires a Synapse account in a person's name with acceptance of Synapse's terms of use [S9], [S20]. Both datasets identify participants only by study codes. Our repository contains no recordings, but it does contain derived tables with one row per participant: the dataset's code, diagnosis, age, sex, sub-study or site, stride counts, the timing features and the models' predictions. [For the group: confirm that the Synapse terms allow sharing these derived per-participant tables; CC BY 4.0 itself allows sharing with attribution.] Neither sample represents a screening population. Both compare diagnosed patients with volunteer controls, and men make up about two thirds of the patients in both. gaitpdb records no ethnicity or socioeconomic information [S7], and WearGait-PD records race but nothing socioeconomic [S9]. WearGait-PD's controls are older than its patients, the reverse of gaitpdb, and include people with mild movement disorders such as essential tremor [S9]. Medication state was not controlled in WearGait-PD [S9] and is not recorded in gaitpdb [S7], and the clinical diagnosis that labels both datasets is itself imperfect [S1].

## D. From force signals to features

A foot is in contact while its total force exceeds 5% of the recording's force level, defined as the mean over the two feet of each foot's 95th percentile of total force; contact and swing phases shorter than 0.1 s are merged into their neighbours. Heel strike and toe off are the upward and downward crossings, from which each stride's stride, stance and swing times follow. The threshold was chosen on agreement with WearGait-PD's walkway alone, not on classification results. Of 14 candidates (fixed forces of 10 to 100 N, and 2.5% to 10% of body weight or of the recording's force level), 5% of the force level and 30 N matched the most footfalls one to one with a stance time within 30 ms of the walkway's (46.8% of 19,200 straight-walking footfalls from 170 participants). 5% was kept for its narrower limits of agreement and its higher share of one-to-one matches (83.4% against 79.7%). This criterion replaced a first one, the share matched one to one, after an initial run showed that share rising with the threshold while every stance shortened. The midterm plan's fixed 20 N is kept as a sensitivity analysis.

Turns are removed differently in the two datasets, because only WearGait-PD labels them. In gaitpdb, a turn is expected every corridor length divided by the participant's walking speed (25 m in Ga and Ju [S4], [S6], an assumed 35 m in Si [S19]); where a walk spans at least 2.5 such intervals, the walk's own turn rhythm, the peak of a Lomb-Scargle periodogram of its stride series between 0.7 and 1.5 times the expected interval, sets the spacing instead. A predicted turn is confirmed by a stride at least 10% longer than its foot's median, with the other foot also at least 5% off its median, within 0.3 intervals of the prediction. Every stride overlapping the 2 s either side of a confirmed turn is removed, plus one stride either side, as is any stride 1.75 to 2.25 times its foot's median while the other foot is undisturbed (two strides merged by a missed heel strike). In WearGait-PD, a stride is kept only if every sample is labelled "Walk"; strides touching a "Turn" label are removed without an extra stride either side, gaps of up to 50 ms in the insole signal are filled by linear interpolation, and strides touching longer gaps are removed. In both datasets a stride must last 0.5 to 2.5 s with swing between 10% and 70% of it, and the first and last stride of each foot in each recording are dropped; in WearGait-PD a recording is a whole task file, so the ends of the straight bouts within it are kept.

Five features are computed per participant from the clean strides of both feet: mean stride time; stride-time and swing-time variability, each the coefficient of variation (100 × SD / mean) computed per foot and averaged over the feet; swing fraction, the mean swing time as a share of its stride; and swing-time asymmetry, 100 × |left − right| / mean of the two feet's mean swing times. The midterm plan's sixth feature, stride-time asymmetry, was dropped before any WearGait-PD label was used, because in a continuous walk it is near zero by construction and mostly reflects how many strides a participant has.

## E. Models

The main models use the five gait features. A logistic regression with an L2 penalty standardises its inputs, enters swing-time asymmetry as log(1 + x), and chooses its penalty strength among 17 values from 10⁻⁴ to 10⁴ by an inner five-fold cross-validation on log-loss within each training set. A random forest [S22] of 300 trees chooses its maximum depth (2, 3, 5 or unlimited) and the number of inputs tried at each split (1, the rounded square root of the number of inputs, or all of them) by the log-loss of its out-of-bag predictions within each training set. [A support vector machine is added under the same evaluation when task T-020 lands.] For comparison, both models are also fitted on the gait features with age and sex, and the logistic regression on age and sex alone.

## F. Evaluation

The primary measure is the area under the ROC curve (AUC), which scores how well a model ranks people with PD above controls without fixing a threshold or a prevalence, neither of which is settled for screening. The research question asks whether the separation holds for participants, protocols and cohorts a model has not seen, so the models are evaluated at four levels (Fig. 2). For unseen participants ("within protocol"), five-fold cross-validation of gaitpdb, stratified by diagnosis and grouped by participant, is repeated 20 times; the AUC is the mean over the 100 test folds, with a 95% interval corrected for the overlap between training sets [S17]. For an unseen protocol ("across protocols"), each gaitpdb sub-study is held out in turn, and the AUC is computed on the three held-out sets of predictions pooled, with a 95% participant bootstrap interval (2,000 resamples); the drop between the levels is the AUC of the pooled out-of-fold predictions (averaged over the 20 repeats) minus this one, with a paired bootstrap interval. The combined analysis, the main analysis of our research plan ("pooled" in the output files), runs the same repeated cross-validation over all 324 participants, stratified by diagnosis and by dataset, and also scores the AUC within each dataset's test participants. For an unseen cohort, the cross-cohort test, each model is fitted once on all of gaitpdb, with all tuning inside gaitpdb, and applied once to WearGait-PD, with a 95% participant bootstrap interval.

The cross-cohort analysis and its checks were committed before any model was scored against WearGait-PD's diagnoses. The checks (Table II) vary the inputs (standardised within each dataset, without the two variability features, with robust variability measures, with age and sex), rerun the cleaning, the features and both the within-gaitpdb logistic regression and the cross-cohort test at a fixed 20 N, and restrict the test to VA Puget Sound or to pairs of a patient and a control of the same sex within three years of age, matched greedily from the youngest patient. Two further checks never score predictions against WearGait-PD's diagnoses: whether a model can tell the datasets apart from the five features, and a stride-matched reference, gaitpdb's within-protocol AUC when each test participant's features are recomputed from as many clean strides per foot as a randomly drawn WearGait-PD participant has, taken as runs from 13 stretches of the walk (WearGait-PD's median number of straight bouts per foot), with the models trained on full-length features (20 draws).

The comparisons are chance, age and sex alone, and each feature alone (a logistic regression on that feature, fitted on the same splits). Paired differences in AUC use the corrected repeated cross-validation t-test within protocol [S18], with Holm's adjustment [S23], and a paired bootstrap across protocols. To see which features carry the separation, we report each feature's rank AUC over all participants, the odds ratios per standard deviation of the logistic regression fitted on all 155 gaitpdb participants (1,000-resample participant bootstrap, with variance inflation factors), and the random forest's permutation importance, the drop in test-fold AUC when one input is shuffled among the test participants (10 shuffles, averaged over the five folds of one cross-validation repeat). To show what an operating point would mean, we report balanced accuracy at a probability of 0.5; sensitivity at 90% specificity, read off the ROC curve of the pooled out-of-fold predictions within gaitpdb and of the predictions in WearGait-PD; the specificity and sensitivity in WearGait-PD of the threshold that gave 90% specificity on gaitpdb's out-of-fold predictions; and the predictive values at a prevalence of 1%, the prevalence of PD over the age of 60 [S15], which overstates it for people aged 50 or over. Checks written after the cross-cohort result was known, such as models trained within WearGait-PD and the relation of the predictions to age, are reported separately as exploratory.
