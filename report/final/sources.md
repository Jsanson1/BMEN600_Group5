| S21 | D. Kroneberg et al., "Less is more – estimation of the number of strides required to assess gait variability in spatially confined settings," *Front. Aging Neurosci.*, vol. 10, Art. no. 435, Jan. 2019, doi: 10.3389/fnagi.2018.00435. | 2026-11-02: bibliographic record at the Max Delbrück Center repository (edoc.mdc-berlin.de/18023: 11 authors led by D. Kroneberg, published 21 January 2019, CC BY 4.0) and the full text (the repository's open-access PDF) | Discussion, walking protocol: inertial sensors, 31 patients with movement disorders (12 PD, 7 ataxia, 12 essential tremor) walking a 10 m segment back and forth, and 162 healthy older adults on a 20 m segment. Strides after 180° turns were "spatially shorter and temporally longer" than the walk average, by 2.8% and 3.2% in the patients and 1.2% and 1.4% in the healthy older adults; the changes were "specifically prevalent in PD and ET groups" (no PD-only values in the main text). Removing one more stride before and after each turn changed group mean variability by up to 0.5% in the patient groups and under 0.2% in the healthy group, "of negligible magnitude at group level". About 15 strides sufficed for reliable variability estimates in these groups (18 to 20 strides for the stride time coefficient of variation at R > 0.8). |
