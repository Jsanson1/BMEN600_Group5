# AI-assisted work: the full account

The final paper's AI-Assisted Work statement is a short version (in `report/final/statements.md`), chosen on 16 November to fit the page limit (`DECISIONS.md` D-015). This is the detailed account it points to, moved here unchanged from draft 3 of the statements (task T-034). Text in square brackets is a placeholder for the member named in it; each member checks the part about themselves. Citations use the labels of `report/midterm/sources.md` and `report/final/sources.md`.

The main AI tool was Claude (Anthropic; mostly the Claude Opus 5.5 model), used through Claude Code in a terminal, the Claude desktop app and cloud sessions. Every transformation of the data is in the committed code; no AI tool labelled, edited or analysed data outside it.

For Jamie, Claude designed and wrote most of the analysis code (gait-event detection, the features, the shared evaluation, both models, the model comparison, the analyses across the two datasets and the check of the contact threshold against WearGait-PD's walkway), the figures and tables, and the repository's documentation and collaboration protocol, and ran the analyses. From its exploratory runs it proposed several processing choices, among them the contact threshold, dropping stride-time asymmetry and the added checks of the cross-cohort test, which Jamie put to the group and the group adopted (`DECISIONS.md` D-012, D-013). It searched for and checked literature, downloaded WearGait-PD with Jamie's approval of each step, and drafted the abstract, the Introduction (from Anna's midterm Introduction and Jamie's midterm Background), the Methods, Results, Discussion and Conclusion and these statements; the abstract and Conclusion were drafted at the team's request on 16 November, in place of Yassien, so that the paper could be finished. Separate Claude agents, given only the drafts, the output files and the source records, reviewed the Results, Discussion and Methods drafts, and on 16 November the code of the cross-dataset analyses (pull request #22) and the assembled paper; each finding was checked before anything was changed. Jamie skimmed the code, figures and section drafts of 13 November for errors.

For Anna, Claude Code wrote and ran the exploratory analyses of the stride rules, the turn detection and the stride counts; wrote the shared cleaning module and its tests; and wrote the support vector machine, its tests and its evaluation script. Anna directed the exploration. She questioned whether the relative stride rule was arbitrary or biased against PD; proposed the spectral analysis, the stride-to-stride differences and the Butterworth filter; and found and read the Si paper that established its walking condition. She chose the methods: timing-based turn detection rather than filtering, removing the strides either side of a turn, interpolation of short gaps, and the turn detector's phase check and its tolerance. After the tests exposed problems, Claude added four refinements to the turn check, which Anna approved after seeing the results. For the SVM, the kernels and search range follow her midterm plan, and she chose to tune the kernel together with C and gamma, and the grid spacing. She read the three sub-study papers herself to verify the corridor lengths, and reviewed the scripts and texts before they were committed. For the midterm, Anna wrote the Introduction and Methods herself. Claude brainstormed methods, drafted the statistical-analysis phrasing in the Methods and quality-checked both sections; Anna checked their wording against the cited papers.

For Yassien, for the midterm, Claude drafted the dataset section from the PhysioNet page and the WearGait-PD article over five rounds of review, drafted the project plan and its table, renumbered the reference list and checked two references against their Crossref and DataCite records. For the final project, Claude (in the desktop app) checked his WearGait-PD download against the team's checksums, set up and watched his rerun of the whole analysis, and compared every output with the committed one; on 13 November it reran the corrected threshold check (pull request #15) and Anna's support vector machine on his PC and compared their outputs with the committed ones. Yassien downloaded the data with his own account, ran the analyses on his machine and decided what to report. His reviews of the threshold check (#15) and of the features, random forest and model comparison (#16 to #18) were each made with Claude's help: he ran the tests and, where the data were on his machine, reran the scripts and compared their outputs with the committed ones, as each review on GitHub records.

Every reference was checked against its DOI, PubMed, publisher or bibliographic record, and `report/midterm/sources.md` and `report/final/sources.md` record how each was checked and what it supports. Every number, table and figure is produced by a script in the repository, and unit tests (150 at the submitted version) check the shared code and the scripts. A final rerun of every step from a clean copy of the repository at `02e69c9` (16 November; the submitted version adds only the paper's tooling to that code) rewrote 83 of the 106 committed outputs, every one byte for byte, apart from the line in each of the two `features_summary.txt` files that records the commit; the other 23 are the exploration outputs and the record of the download, which `run_all.py` does not rewrite. It ran in parts of under two hours with the scratch versions of `scripts/run_all_in_parts.py` and `scripts/run_cross_cohort_cached.py`, and the committed `run_cross_cohort_cached.py`, run on the same kept results, rewrote the cross-dataset outputs identically. Yassien's rerun of the whole analysis on a second machine (Windows, newer NumPy and pandas), at `87b45f5` before the support vector machine was added, reproduced every number then reported, with differences only in line endings, in last digits (at most 1e-13) and in image bytes but not pixels; and the numbers in the drafts of the Results and Discussion were checked against the output files (a script listed every number not found in them, and each listed number was traced by hand), and on 16 November the numbers of the assembled paper were checked again, by script and by an independent review that traced each to its model, analysis, dataset and group. The pull requests with the features, models, cleaning and threshold check were reviewed by another member before merging (D-003), often after a first pass by that member's Claude: Yassien reviewed the features, random forest and model comparison (pull requests #16 to #18) and, after rerunning it, the WearGait-PD threshold check (#15); Anna reviewed the threshold check too (#15, where her review led to a corrected check); Jamie reviewed the stride cleaning (#21). The pull request with the cross-dataset analyses and the final rerun (#22) was merged on 16 November after the independent review above instead of a member's (D-016), and the midterm's pull requests, mostly snapshots and documentation, were merged by their author. Anna's support vector machine was rerun in Jamie's cloud session and on Yassien's PC, and its summary was identical each time; in the final rerun from a clean copy at `02e69c9` (16 November), every one of its output files was identical to the committed one.
